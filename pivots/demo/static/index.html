Executed Pivots

NVIDIA's Terminal-Pivot rewards an agent's next terminal step by how closely its keystrokes match a teacher's. Here you write that step. The server forks the terminal at that moment, runs your batch in the fork, and scores it two ways: J, the shipped string reward, and X, which compares what your batch did with what the expert's did.

J is an exact port of NeMo Gym's terminus_judge in its string-only setting: the batch's keystrokes are joined and compared with the teacher's by difflib.SequenceMatcher; it pays 1 at a ratio of 0.9 or more. Nothing runs.

X runs the expert's batch and yours from the same checkpoint and compares files created, modified and deleted (by content hash), the terminal output, the working directory and exported variables, with penalties for side effects, destructive deletes, new errors, timeouts and a premature task_complete. It pays 1 at an effect score of 0.75 or more. P asks the task's own verifier how many of its checks pass right after your step, compared with after the expert's.

The six tasks are specimens we wrote; the stored students are samples from two Claude models standing in for Nemotron Nano. Worlds are forked with Linux user namespaces and overlayfs: no network, no capabilities, a time limit per batch. Nothing you type is stored.
